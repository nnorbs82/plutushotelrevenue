WHAT IS IN THE PLATFORM

Cloudbeds is trying to make the connected stack the product.

The platform proposition goes well beyond the PMS. The attraction for an independent hotel is reducing duplicate work between reservations, distribution, direct booking, payments, reporting and increasingly marketing and revenue tools.

BEFORE MIGRATION

Four things I would prove.

01

Run real room-type, rate-plan, restriction and group scenarios rather than the clean standard demo workflow.

02

Check whether the reporting layer gives revenue and management teams what they need without recreating everything in spreadsheets.

03

Map every distribution connection and booking-engine dependency before migration so commercial gaps do not appear after go-live.

04

Decide where platform breadth creates genuine simplicity and where a specialist system still offers meaningfully better depth.

WHERE I WOULD TEST FIT

Cloudbeds makes most sense when the hotel values consolidation.

01

Independent hotels wanting PMS, distribution and direct-booking workflows in one environment should test the integrated experience carefully.

02

Hotels with groups, events or other bookable spaces should test the newer commercial workflows against their real sales process.

03

Revenue teams should validate rate controls, reporting depth, pickup views and export needs before assuming the unified platform removes spreadsheets.

04

If specialist systems are already strong, the migration case should identify exactly which tools and manual steps would disappear.

THE QUESTION I WOULD ASK

How much value comes from keeping the stack unified?

The answer depends on the property. Consolidation works when it removes duplicate work, improves data flow and preserves commercial control. It is less valuable when important workflows have to be compromised just to stay inside one platform.
