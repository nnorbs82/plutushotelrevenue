Hotel digital marketing

Create demand without making a discount the product.

I think hotel marketing works best when positioning, content, distribution and revenue strategy support each other. A campaign can fill a need period, but a permanent promotion can quietly become the normal public rate.

01

Separate demand capture from demand creation

OTAs and paid search are often excellent at capturing demand that already exists. Content, partnerships, events and targeted campaigns can create or redirect demand. I would judge them differently.

  • Define what each channel is supposed to achieve.
  • Match activity to the hotel's booking window.
  • Do not give every booking to the last click.
02

Give every promotion a specific job

A promotion should solve a defined demand problem: a weak date, a specific market, a booking window or a targeted segment. If it never ends, it is no longer a promotion - it is the price.

  • Use clear start and end dates.
  • Target need periods instead of discounting everything.
  • Measure ADR after the discount and commission.
Better

A targeted offer for identified need dates with a measured result.

Riskier

Stacking permanent OTA promotions until the public rate loses meaning.

03

Give direct booking a useful reason to exist

I prefer direct-booking benefits that reduce friction or add value without automatically creating a large price gap.

  • Flexible or clearer direct terms.
  • Useful room choice or add-ons.
  • Easy contact and service advantages.
  • Consistent rate messaging across channels.
04

Create content around real travel intent

Generic social posting is not a strategy. Hotel content becomes more useful when it answers why somebody is travelling, what they need to know about the location and what might stop them booking.

  • Local events and destination information.
  • Room-category and experience stories.
  • Practical guides that match search intent.
05

Measure net contribution, not revenue alone

A booking worth €200 is not automatically better than one worth €180 if the acquisition cost, commission, discount, cancellation profile and payment cost are very different.

  • Compare net ADR and acquisition cost by channel.
  • Review cancellation, lead time and length of stay.
  • Include media spend and merchant fees where relevant.

Marketing decision checklist

The questions I would ask before launching another promotion or campaign.

✓What demand problem am I trying to solve?
✓Which dates or audience actually need support?
✓What happens to net ADR after discount and commission?
✓Is the offer genuinely temporary?
✓Could direct booking add value instead of matching the discount?
✓Does the content match real guest intent?
✓Am I measuring conversion and net contribution?
✓Will I know when to stop the campaign?
