Hotel digital marketing

Marketing should create useful demand - not just cheaper bookings.

I look at hotel marketing through a revenue lens. A campaign is only good if the booking it creates is worth more than the discount, commission, media spend and displacement it introduces.

01

Know whether you are creating demand or simply paying to capture it.

A guest searching your hotel name already has high intent. A guest seeing a destination story for the first time does not. Those two moments deserve different budgets, messages and expectations.

Useful distinction
  • Brand search captures existing intent
  • Destination content can create consideration
  • Retargeting follows existing interest
Common mistake
  • Calling all attributed bookings incremental
  • Paying commission on demand already secured
  • Using one KPI for every channel
02

Every promotion should have a specific job and an end date.

A promotion can help a weak Sunday or a soft booking window. The problem starts when the discounted price becomes permanent and guests learn that the headline rate is never the real rate.

Good promotion
  • Targets a defined need period
  • Has clear start and end dates
  • Is measured against net ADR
Danger signs
  • Promotion runs all year
  • Stacks with multiple other discounts
  • Performance is judged only on occupancy
03

Give direct booking a reason that is useful, not just cheaper.

Direct value can be better flexibility, clearer room choice, a practical add-on or easier contact. Competing only on price can start a discount race the hotel cannot win profitably.

Useful direct benefits
  • More flexible cancellation
  • Room preference or priority
  • Parking / breakfast value where sensible
Avoid
  • Heavy direct discount plus paid acquisition
  • Benefits that cost more than commission saved
  • Different promises across channels
04

Content should answer a travel question or create a reason to stay.

Generic social posts rarely move the commercial needle. Useful content connects the hotel to the reasons people travel: events, neighbourhoods, practical logistics, room experiences and seasonal demand.

Worth creating
  • Local event and transport guides
  • Room and stay explanations
  • Destination content with search intent
Low-value content
  • Generic motivational quotes
  • Unrelated trend-chasing posts
  • Content with no path back to booking
05

Revenue can look good while the booking is commercially poor.

Look beyond gross revenue. Include discount, OTA commission, media spend, payment fees, cancellation behaviour and the chance that the room would have sold anyway.

Measure
  • Net ADR
  • Acquisition cost
  • Cancellation rate
  • Lead time and need-date fit
Do not stop at
  • ROAS without margin context
  • Bookings attributed by last click
  • Revenue before discount and commission

Before launching a campaign

Hotel marketing checklist

  • Clear need period or commercial objective
  • Target audience defined
  • Promotion has an end date
  • Discount stacking checked
  • OTA commission included in economics
  • Media spend included in economics
  • Payment cost considered
  • Direct alternative compared
  • Cancellation conditions checked
  • Booking window matches demand problem
  • Success metric defined before launch
  • Post-campaign net contribution reviewed
