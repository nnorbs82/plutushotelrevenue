Tips / Digital marketing

Digital Marketing Tips for Hotels

Build visibility, communicate directly and create measurable demand - without losing sight of what every booking costs and contributes.

01 · Start with discoverability and search visibility02 · Use digital channels for direct communication03 · Use personalisation to improve the guest experience04 · Measure marketing against commercial value05 · Use analytics to learn, not only to report06 · Build a balanced digital marketing mix07 · Do not let promotions become the product

The original Plutus marketing guide focused on visibility, direct communication, customer experience, cost effectiveness and measurable results. Those principles still hold. The important addition is a stronger commercial lens: marketing should create or capture demand at an acquisition cost the hotel understands.

01

Start with discoverability and search visibility

Digital marketing begins with being findable. A hotel website should have technically sound pages, clear destination relevance and useful content that matches the questions travellers search for. Search Engine Optimization is not a one-time keyword exercise; it is the combined effect of site quality, useful content, local signals and a property that is represented consistently across the web.

Paid search can complement organic visibility when the hotel knows what demand it is trying to capture. Brand campaigns, destination terms and retargeting all behave differently, so the objective and the cost of the booking should be clear before spend increases.

Practical tip: Separate demand that the hotel created from demand it was already likely to receive. That distinction matters when judging return.

02

Use digital channels for direct communication

Social platforms give a hotel a direct line to potential and previous guests. They are useful for showing atmosphere, events, seasonal moments, people and experiences that do not always fit naturally on a booking page. They also make reputation visible, so responses to comments and reviews become part of the marketing.

The goal is not to post every day simply to satisfy an algorithm. A smaller amount of useful, recognisable content can build a stronger brand than a feed filled with generic destination quotes or stock images.

Practical tip: Choose content themes the hotel can sustain - rooms, people, food, local life, events and behind-the-scenes moments are usually stronger than generic inspiration.

03

Use personalisation to improve the guest experience

Digital marketing can continue after the first click. Email, pre-arrival communication, segmented offers and repeat-guest messages allow the hotel to speak differently to different audiences. A business traveller may value ease and flexibility; a weekend leisure guest may respond to an experience or package.

Personalisation should remain useful rather than invasive. Use the data the guest has legitimately provided to make communication more relevant, and keep frequency under control.

Practical tip: A message is only personalised if the content changes meaningfully for the recipient. Adding a first name to the same promotion is not enough.

04

Measure marketing against commercial value

Digital channels can be far more targeted than traditional broad advertising, but low cost per click does not automatically mean profitable marketing. Follow the booking beyond the click. Consider conversion, cancellation, rate, commission avoided, campaign cost and whether the booking displaced demand that would have arrived anyway.

For hotels, the strongest marketing metric is rarely engagement alone. Reach and clicks are useful diagnostics, but the commercial question is what the activity contributed to revenue and at what acquisition cost.

Practical tip: Compare acquisition cost as a percentage of booking revenue so paid direct demand can be judged against OTA commission and other channel costs.

05

Use analytics to learn, not only to report

Website traffic, conversion rate, booking-engine exits, campaign performance and engagement can reveal where the journey is working or breaking. Measurement is valuable because it allows the hotel to stop doing things that are not producing a result and invest more confidently in what is.

Set the measurement plan before the campaign starts. If the goal is direct revenue, make sure the booking engine and analytics can attribute that revenue. If the goal is awareness for a new product, use appropriate leading indicators but define when the activity will be expected to translate into bookings.

Practical tip: A dashboard is not a strategy. Every recurring metric should have a reason for being watched and a possible action attached to it.

06

Build a balanced digital marketing mix

Hotels can combine SEO, social media, content marketing, email, influencer partnerships and pay-per-click advertising. Each serves a different role. SEO and useful content can build long-term visibility. Social helps the property stay recognisable and human. Email supports relationships with people the hotel already knows. Paid media can capture or stimulate specific demand quickly. Influencers can provide reach and creative content when the audience fit is genuine.

The mix should reflect the hotel, the market and the resources available. A small independent property does not need to be excellent on every channel. It needs a few channels that are maintained well, measured properly and connected to a clear commercial objective.

Practical tip: Write the purpose of each channel in one sentence. If two channels are doing exactly the same job, the budget or effort may be unnecessarily fragmented.

07

Do not let promotions become the product

Discounts can create conversion, but constant promotions can also teach guests that the displayed rate is never the real rate. This is particularly important on OTAs where mobile, member, country and campaign discounts may stack. More bookings are not automatically better if net ADR and room revenue weaken.

Use promotions for a defined problem - a need period, a target audience, a market-entry objective or a measurable campaign - and review the net result. When the promotion finishes, the hotel should know whether it created incremental demand or simply discounted bookings that would have arrived anyway.

Practical tip: Before launching a discount, write down what success looks like, how long the promotion will run and which metric will decide whether it stays.

Good hotel marketing does not only generate attention. It creates demand the hotel can recognise in the commercial result.
