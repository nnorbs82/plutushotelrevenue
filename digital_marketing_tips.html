DIGITAL MARKETING / COMMERCIAL DEMAND

Attention is
not the result.

Hotel marketing should create or capture demand at a cost the hotel understands. Reach, clicks and engagement are signals. The commercial result is what remains after acquisition cost, channel cost, cancellation and displacement are considered.

01

Start with discoverability.

A hotel's digital marketing starts with being findable. The website should have technically sound pages, clear destination relevance and useful content that matches the questions travellers actually search for. SEO is not a one-time keyword exercise - it is the combined effect of site quality, content, local signals and consistent representation across the web.

Paid search can complement organic visibility when the hotel knows what demand it is trying to capture. Brand campaigns, destination terms and retargeting behave differently, so the objective and booking cost should be clear before spend increases.

COMMERCIAL CHECK: Separate demand the campaign created from demand the hotel was already likely to receive.
02

Use social to stay recognisable, not merely active.

Social platforms are useful for showing atmosphere, events, seasonal moments, people and experiences that do not always fit naturally on a booking page. They also make reputation visible, so responses to comments and reviews become part of the marketing.

The objective is not to post every day simply to satisfy an algorithm. A smaller amount of recognisable content can build a stronger brand than a feed full of generic destination quotes and stock imagery.

COMMERCIAL CHECK: Choose content themes the hotel can sustain - rooms, people, food, local life, events and behind-the-scenes moments.
03

Change the message when the audience changes.

Email, pre-arrival communication, segmented offers and repeat-guest messages allow the hotel to speak differently to different audiences. A business traveller may value flexibility and efficiency; a leisure guest may respond to experience, occasion or package value.

Personalisation should remain useful rather than invasive. Use legitimately collected data to make communication more relevant and keep frequency under control.

COMMERCIAL CHECK: Adding a first name to the same promotion is not meaningful personalisation.
04

Measure marketing against net value.

A low cost per click does not automatically mean profitable marketing. Follow the booking beyond the click. Consider conversion, cancellation, rate, commission avoided, campaign cost and whether the booking displaced demand that would have arrived anyway.

Reach and engagement are useful diagnostics, but the stronger hotel question is what the activity contributed to rooms revenue and at what acquisition cost.

COMMERCIAL CHECK: Compare acquisition cost as a percentage of booking revenue so paid direct demand can be judged against OTA commission and other channel costs.
05

Analytics should change a decision.

Website traffic, conversion rate, booking-engine exits, campaign performance and engagement can reveal where the journey is working or breaking. Measurement is useful because it lets the hotel stop doing things that do not produce a result and invest more confidently in what does.

Set the measurement plan before the campaign begins. If the objective is direct revenue, make sure the booking engine and analytics can attribute that revenue. If the goal is awareness, define when and how the activity is expected to translate into bookings.

COMMERCIAL CHECK: Every recurring metric should have a reason for being watched and a possible action attached to it.
06

You do not need to be excellent everywhere.

SEO, social, content, email, influencer partnerships and paid advertising each serve different roles. SEO and useful content can build long-term visibility. Social helps the hotel remain recognisable and human. Email supports relationships with people the property already knows. Paid media can capture or stimulate specific demand quickly.

The mix should reflect the hotel, the market and the resources available. A small independent property needs a few channels maintained well, measured properly and tied to a clear commercial objective - not a weak presence on every platform.

COMMERCIAL CHECK: Write the purpose of every channel in one sentence. If two channels are doing exactly the same job, effort may be unnecessarily fragmented.
07

Do not let the discount become the product.

Discounts can increase conversion, but permanent promotions can teach guests that the displayed rate is never the real rate. On OTAs, mobile, member, country and campaign discounts may stack. More bookings are not automatically better if net ADR and accommodation revenue weaken.

Use promotions for a defined problem - a need period, target audience, market-entry objective or measurable campaign - and review the net result. When the promotion ends, the hotel should know whether it created incremental demand or simply discounted bookings that would have arrived anyway.

COMMERCIAL CHECK: Before launching a discount, define the problem, the duration and the metric that will decide whether it stays.

Good hotel marketing does not only generate attention. It creates demand the hotel can recognise in the commercial result.
