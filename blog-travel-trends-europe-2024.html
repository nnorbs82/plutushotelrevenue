Travel Trends for Europe in 2024: Sustainability, Technology and Authentic Experiences

07 SEP 2024 · PLUTUS HOTEL REVENUE

European travel in 2024 continued to be shaped by a mix of familiar demand and changing expectations. Guests wanted convenience, but not necessarily sameness. They looked for memorable experiences, better digital journeys and clearer signs that destinations and businesses were thinking about their impact.

Sustainable exploration

Environmental awareness influenced transport, accommodation and destination choices for part of the market. Rail travel, lower-impact itineraries and properties with credible sustainability practices gained attention.

For hotels, this was a reminder to make practical information visible: public transport, cycling, walkability, local suppliers and genuine operational initiatives can all strengthen the guest proposition.

Technology becomes expected infrastructure

Mobile-friendly booking, digital payment, messaging and fast access to information increasingly felt less like innovation and more like basic service design.

The challenge for hotels was to use technology without making the experience impersonal. Automation is strongest when it removes repetitive friction and leaves staff more time for the interactions that actually need a person.

Authenticity becomes a commercial differentiator

Travellers continued to seek neighbourhoods, food, culture and experiences that felt connected to the destination. Independent hotels are well placed to benefit because they can often tell a more specific local story than a standardised brand.

That advantage needs to be visible online. Local recommendations, partnerships, photography and packages can help a guest understand why staying at the hotel changes the trip.

Shoulder seasons gain attention

Crowding, heat and price pressure in peak periods can shift some travellers toward spring, autumn and quieter dates. Revenue managers should watch whether booking curves and market segments are changing rather than relying on old definitions of high and low season.

A trend only becomes useful when it is visible in the property's own pace, rate and demand data.
