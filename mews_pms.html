The proposition is bigger than PMS.

Mews has expanded from property management into payments, guest journeys, revenue, intelligence, finance and distribution. The attraction is a tighter operating system with fewer disconnected tools and less distance between the daily hotel and the commercial view.

That breadth is only valuable when the native workflows are deep enough for the property. Consolidation should remove friction rather than simply move more functions into one vendor relationship.

REVENUE-MANAGEMENT READ

Four things I would test.

01

How clearly room categories, rate products and reservation structures translate into the reports and decisions a revenue manager actually uses.

02

Which specialist systems can genuinely be replaced without weakening a workflow the property relies on.

03

How groups, room moves, accounting and non-standard inventory behave under real operational pressure, not only in a demo.

04

Whether the growing commercial layer genuinely shortens the distance between operations and revenue decision-making.

THE QUESTION I WOULD ASK

How much should live in one operating system?

Map the current PMS, payments, booking engine, RMS, BI, messaging, accounting and distribution stack. Then decide which functions Mews can replace while making the hotel genuinely simpler to operate and easier to read commercially.
