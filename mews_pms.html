01 / RESERVATIONS

The reservation record is still the operating foundation.

Room inventory, reservations, room moves, rate products and guest profiles are the source data that every other module depends on. For a revenue manager, the important question is whether room-category and rate structures remain clear enough to analyse commercially.

Core PMSInventory, reservations, rooms and guest records
Revenue testCan the commercial structure be read cleanly?
02 / PAYMENTS

Money sits inside the guest workflow rather than beside it.

Mews places payments close to the reservation lifecycle. For hotels that currently split PMS and payment work across multiple systems, that can reduce hand-offs - but finance teams still need to test reconciliation, exceptions, refunds and accounting reality rather than only the guest-facing path.

AutomationPayment actions linked to reservation events
Hotel testReconciliation and exception handling
03 / HOUSEKEEPING + OPS

The software has to survive the messy hotel day.

Clean demos are easy. Real hotels have room changes, maintenance blocks, late departures, early arrivals, special inventory and last-minute exceptions. This is where the operational design of a PMS either becomes invisible - or becomes work.

Daily useRoom status, task flow and operational visibility
Stress testUse the hotel's real exception scenarios
04 / GUEST JOURNEY

A modern PMS increasingly touches the guest directly.

Digital check-in, messaging, self-service and guest communication make the operating platform part of the experience, not only the back office. The value is strongest when automation removes friction without making the hotel feel inaccessible.

Guest-facingDigital journey and communication
Design testAutomation with a clear human route
05 / REVENUE

Mews is moving pricing into the native operating environment.

The 2026 operating-system direction includes native revenue management. That changes the evaluation: a hotel can compare an external specialist RMS against the benefit of pricing logic that sits closer to live PMS data and fewer integrations.

Native RMSDynamic pricing inside the Mews ecosystem
Revenue testDepth, controls, transparency and exception handling
06 / BI + FINANCE

The platform is expanding into management and finance work.

Business intelligence, accounting, receivables and recurring-payment tools push Mews beyond front-office software. For a hotel, the real value is whether these additions reduce external reporting and finance work without weakening the depth required by management and accounting.

2026 directionBI, accounting, AR and recurring payments
Management testWhat work genuinely disappears outside Mews?
07 / DISTRIBUTION

The operating system is reaching outward to demand.

Mews Channel Manager, powered by SiteMinder, brings distribution closer to the same platform. The hotel should still evaluate mappings, restrictions, channel controls and total economics, but the architectural appeal is clear: less distance between the PMS record and the channels selling it.

Native layerChannel management within the Mews relationship
Commercial testControl, connectivity and total distribution cost
MY HOTEL FIT TEST

Do not buy the ecosystem. Prove the workflow.

01 / FIT

Hotels wanting cloud-native operations and greater automation should test Mews deeply rather than judging only the interface.

02 / CONSOLIDATE

Map PMS, payments, RMS, BI, messaging, finance and distribution. Identify exactly what can be retired and what remains specialist.

03 / STRESS

Demonstrate groups, room moves, accounting, unusual inventory and real hotel exceptions before migration.

04 / ECONOMICS

Compare total platform cost and implementation effort against the systems and manual processes that genuinely disappear.
