WHAT IS IN THE PLATFORM

Mews is no longer just the front-desk system.

The 2026 direction is a unified operating system: the PMS core remains important, but Mews is pulling more commercial, financial and guest-facing work into the same data environment. For a hotel, the question becomes which parts of the wider stack can genuinely be consolidated.

01 / PMS + OPERATIONS

Reservations & operations

Inventory, reservations, room moves, housekeeping and the daily operating record remain the foundation.

02 / PAYMENTS + FINANCE

Money inside the workflow

Payments sit close to the reservation flow, while 2026 expansion includes accounting, accounts receivable, recurring payments and wider financial tooling.

03 / REVENUE

Native RMS

Mews RMS brings automated dynamic pricing into the platform rather than relying only on an external revenue system.

04 / DISTRIBUTION

Native channel management

Mews Channel Manager is powered by SiteMinder and is positioned as a native distribution layer connecting to 400+ OTAs.

05 / INTELLIGENCE

BI & reporting

The operating-system direction includes native business intelligence so operational and commercial data can sit closer together.

06 / ECOSYSTEM

Specialist integrations still matter

Mews also reports 1,000+ hospitality integrations, so hotels are not forced to abandon specialist systems where they still create value.

MY REVENUE-MANAGEMENT READ

Four things I would prove before choosing it.

01

How clearly room categories, rate products and reservation structures translate into the reports and decisions a revenue manager actually uses.

02

Which specialist systems can genuinely be replaced without weakening a workflow the property relies on.

03

How groups, room moves, accounting and non-standard inventory behave under real operational pressure rather than only in a clean demo path.

04

Whether the growing commercial layer genuinely shortens the distance between operations and revenue decision-making.

WHERE I WOULD TEST FIT

The right Mews hotel is not defined by room count alone.

01

Hotels wanting cloud-native operations and a strong automation mindset should explore the platform deeply.

02

Properties hoping to consolidate payments, RMS, messaging, BI or distribution should compare native depth against their current specialist stack.

03

Complex groups, accounting, unusual inventory and multi-property workflows should be demonstrated with the hotel's real scenarios before migration.

04

The commercial case should include the total platform cost, implementation work and the systems that can truly be retired - not only the PMS subscription.

THE QUESTION I WOULD ASK

How much should live in one operating system?

Map the current PMS, payments, booking engine, RMS, BI, messaging, accounting and distribution stack. Then decide which functions Mews can replace while making the hotel genuinely simpler to operate and easier to read commercially.
