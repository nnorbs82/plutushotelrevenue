01 / First screen

Tell me what the hotel is before asking me to scroll.

The opening screen should answer three things quickly: what kind of property is this, where is it, and why should I care? The booking action should be visible without fighting the visual hierarchy.

Do

Use a specific headline, one strong truthful image and a clear booking action.

Avoid

Generic slogans that could belong to any hotel, plus multiple competing buttons above the fold.

02 / Guest questions

Structure pages around decisions, not hotel departments.

Guests do not think in your internal structure. They think: Which room suits me? Where do I park? Is breakfast included? Can I bring a child? How far is the station?

  • Rooms and exact differences.
  • Location, transport and parking.
  • Breakfast, facilities and practical information.
  • Policies that affect the booking decision.
  • Useful local reasons to stay.

03 / Room comparison

If guests cannot see the difference, they will choose on price.

Room-category pages should make the commercial ladder obvious. If Superior is €25 more than Standard, the page should make the extra value visible without making the guest decode marketing language.

Show clearly

Size, bed setup, occupancy, bathroom, view, layout, kitchenette, sofa bed and important limitations.

Do not rely on

Names like Classic, Deluxe or Premium without concrete differences.

04 / Booking friction

Every extra doubt is another chance to return to an OTA.

The route from inspiration to availability should be short, especially on mobile.

  • Keep a persistent booking action.
  • Make rate conditions readable before the guest commits.
  • Keep the booking engine visually connected to the website.
  • Avoid forcing account creation for a simple room booking.
  • Test the complete mobile journey yourself.

05 / Measurement

Page views do not tell you whether the website is doing its job.

Measure the actions that sit closer to revenue.

  • Booking-engine entries.
  • Date searches.
  • Room/rate selection.
  • Completed bookings.
  • Conversion by device and source.
  • Drop-off between website and booking engine.
