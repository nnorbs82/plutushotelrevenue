Hotel website guide

Make the next step
feel obvious.

A hotel website is part of the booking process, not a digital brochure. Every screen should help a guest understand the property, reduce uncertainty and move closer to a decision.

01

Win the first screen

Within a few seconds I want to know what the hotel is, where it is and why I should care. Beautiful but vague websites create work for the guest.

Clear

Specific hotel promise + strong visual + obvious booking action.

Friction

Generic lifestyle line + hidden booking button + no location context.

02

Organise around guest questions

Guests do not think in hotel departments. They think: Which room? Is breakfast included? Can I park? How far is the centre? What happens if I cancel?

  • Rooms and exact differences
  • Location and transport
  • Facilities and practical information
  • Policies and booking conditions
03

Make room categories comparable

If two categories cost €30 apart, the website should make that €30 difference understandable. Show bed configuration, size, bathroom, occupancy, view and important limitations consistently.

04

Remove booking friction

Mobile speed, date selection, rate labels and cancellation terms matter more than another animation. The booking engine should feel like a continuation of the website, not a different company.

Keep

Persistent booking access, readable conditions and simple rate choices.

Remove

Popups, vague inclusions, dead-end pages and unnecessary steps.

05

Measure decisions, not pageviews

I care about availability searches, booking-engine entries, conversion by source and mobile performance. Those tell you whether the website is helping revenue.

Independent hotel revenue management by Norbert Nica.
