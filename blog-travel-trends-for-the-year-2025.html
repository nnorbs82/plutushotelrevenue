Travel Trends for the Year 2025

10 SEP 2024 · PLUTUS HOTEL REVENUE

Travel behaviour changes quickly, but the direction of travel was already clear heading into 2025: guests were becoming more selective about why they travel, how much friction they accept and what kind of experience feels worth paying for.

Sustainability becomes part of the decision

Sustainability moved beyond a niche marketing message. More travellers began looking at transport choices, local impact, energy use and whether a property made responsible decisions visible without turning them into a lecture.

For hotels, the commercial lesson is simple: sustainability works best when it is operationally real and easy for the guest to understand. Clear information about local sourcing, waste reduction, transport access or practical energy measures is stronger than vague green language.

Experiences matter more than generic luxury

Guests increasingly look for a reason to choose one property over another. That reason can be design, location, food, a view, access to local culture, wellness, a distinctive room type or simply a hotel that understands its neighbourhood very well.

Hotels do not need to manufacture an experience. They need to identify what is genuinely distinctive and make it visible in the product description, photography, packages and staff recommendations.

Technology should remove friction

Mobile booking, digital payments, messaging and self-service continued to shape expectations. The useful technology is the technology that makes something easier: finding information, booking, checking in, paying or communicating with the property.

The best digital journey still needs a human fallback. A guest should never feel trapped inside automation when the question becomes specific or important.

Flexible travel patterns continue

Remote and hybrid work made some trips less rigid. Midweek leisure, longer stays and trips that combine work and personal time became more relevant for certain destinations and property types.

That creates revenue opportunities around length-of-stay strategy, workspace, extended-stay products and packages that are built for a real segment rather than a generic discount.

The revenue-management implication

Trends are only useful when they translate into property-level decisions. A hotel should ask which behaviours are visible in its own booking data, which segments are growing, whether booking windows are changing and whether the current product is positioned for that demand.

The market trend is the starting point. The hotel's own data decides whether it deserves action.
