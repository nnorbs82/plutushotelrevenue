Connectivity is still the core proposition.

SiteMinder is fundamentally about moving rates, availability and restrictions from the hotel into a wide distribution landscape and returning reservations without the property manually maintaining each source.

The role becomes more commercially important as the channel mix gets more complex. Distribution technology is not only operational infrastructure - it affects which demand the hotel can reach, how inventory is controlled and what each reservation costs to acquire.

What I would read commercially, not just technically.

01

Pooled inventory and real-time updates reduce manual risk, but the hotel still needs a clear strategy for which channels deserve inventory and when.

02

Yield rules, stop sells and channel controls are valuable only when they reflect a commercial decision rather than being left as static configuration.

03

Channel performance should be compared on net value after commission and promotions, not on gross reservation volume alone.

04

As SiteMinder expands into booking engine, payments, insights and AI-era distribution, hotels should decide which additional functions meaningfully improve the existing stack.

THE QUESTION I WOULD ASK

How complex is the hotel's distribution landscape?

If the property sells through many demand sources, channel management becomes a revenue-management question. The hotel needs to know which channels deserve inventory, what they cost, when their role should change and whether the technology gives enough control to execute that strategy.
