THE COMMERCIAL JOB

Use time before inventory becomes history.

Independent and medium-sized hotels often need strong revenue management without another full on-site management position. An outsourced model can give the property daily commercial attention while the operational team remains focused on the hotel and guest.

The value comes from consistent decisions: reading pickup, understanding the business mix, reacting to demand, adjusting rate, controlling distribution and checking whether performance is improving for the right reasons.

01
BUSINESS MIX / LONG RANGE

Decide which demand deserves the inventory.

Not all occupancy has the same value. Rate, cancellation behaviour, acquisition cost, displacement and operational fit all matter when deciding what mix belongs in the hotel.

  • Balance direct, OTA, corporate, groups, wholesale and tour-operator demand.
  • Identify under-performing segments and practical ways to improve penetration.
  • Create group quotation guidelines and review meaningful opportunities individually.
  • Protect event and compression dates before cheap or restrictive business displaces higher-value demand.
  • Review corporate and tour-operator pricing, availability and actual contribution.
  • Look for lower-cost ways to deliver demand where existing distribution is expensive.
The objective is not maximum occupancy. It is the strongest realistic mix of demand.
02
FORECAST / CREATE TIME

See the likely result while there is still time to change it.

A useful forecast is not a promise. It is an early warning system for strong dates, weak periods, missed assumptions and changing demand patterns.

  • Historical and advanced pickup models where the data supports them.
  • Daily and weekly pace monitoring against previous periods, budget and target.
  • Monitor citywide sell-out dates, fairs, events and unusual demand spikes.
  • Maintain a rolling rooms-revenue forecast for current and forward months.
  • Forecast occupancy, ADR and revenue with variance to budget and last year.
  • Use monthly performance analysis to explain the drivers behind the result.
Forecasting matters because it creates reaction time.
03
YIELD & PRICING / PRESSURE

Price what is left - not what the room sold for yesterday.

Rate should move with demand pressure, remaining inventory, booking window and room-type behaviour rather than with a static calendar.

  • Competitor benchmarking and market-position review.
  • Dynamic pricing using pickup, pace and remaining inventory.
  • Restrictions and targeted promotions for strong and weak periods.
  • Data mining for demand and booking-window trends.
  • Weekday and weekend price-sensitivity review.
  • Room-type yield management and category differentials.
  • Length-of-stay controls where the demand pattern justifies them.
  • Daily pickup analysis with regular strategy adjustment.
A strong rate decision is the rate and condition most likely to maximise the remaining inventory.
04
DISTRIBUTION / NET VALUE

Make sure the strategy reaches the guest without leaking value.

Distribution is more than keeping availability open. Every channel has a different audience, cost, promotion structure and ability to create incremental demand.

  • Use OTA promotions only where they solve a defined commercial problem.
  • Improve content, room mapping, rate plans and merchandising where needed.
  • Review GDS productivity and contracted distribution where relevant.
  • Balance direct and indirect demand rather than treating them as opponents.
  • Identify reservation leakage, mapping problems and rate inconsistencies.
  • Check PMS, channel manager, booking engine and related systems are configured correctly.
  • Measure commission and promotional dilution so performance is read net of cost.
Gross bookings can look impressive while the net contribution tells a weaker story.
05
PERFORMANCE / FEEDBACK LOOP

Close the loop: what happened, why, and what changes next?

Reporting is useful when it changes the next decision. I connect the final result back to pickup, rate decisions, room-type demand, business mix and distribution behaviour.

  • Occupancy, ADR, RevPAR and rooms-revenue performance.
  • Pickup and pace by stay date, booking window and room category.
  • Business mix and channel contribution.
  • Comparison to last year, same-day-last-year, forecast and budget.
  • Net ADR and acquisition-cost considerations where channel cost matters.
  • Identify dates to protect, dates to stimulate and commercial issues that need attention.
The report is finished when the next action is clear.

FAQ

Revenue management without the jargon.

What is revenue management?

Revenue management uses demand data, booking behaviour, market context and commercial controls to make better decisions about pricing, availability and distribution. Hotel room inventory expires every night, so timing is central to the job.

Can remote revenue management work as well as on-site?

Yes, provided the revenue manager has reliable system access, trustworthy data and close communication with the property. Most core revenue work happens through the PMS, distribution systems, market information and regular contact with the hotel team.

What are the main benefits?

More structured pricing, earlier identification of demand changes, stronger forecasting, better control of distribution cost and a clearer understanding of which commercial actions are actually improving performance.

Does revenue management mean constantly raising prices?

No. It also means knowing when the hotel is overpriced, when demand needs stimulation, when a restriction should be removed and when protecting rate is more valuable than chasing occupancy.
