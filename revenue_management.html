THE WORK

More commercial attention. Less hotel administration.

Independent and medium-sized hotels often need strong revenue management without needing another full on-site management position. An outsourced model can give the property daily commercial attention while allowing the operational team to stay focused on the hotel and the guest.

The value comes from consistent decision-making: reading pickup, understanding business mix, reacting to demand, reviewing the forecast, controlling distribution and checking whether the result is improving for the right reasons.

01 / BUSINESS MIX

Not all occupancy has the same value.

The right mix depends on rate, booking behaviour, cancellation risk, acquisition cost, displacement and what each segment contributes over time.

  • Balance direct, OTA, corporate, groups, wholesale and tour-operator demand.
  • Identify under-performing segments and ways to improve penetration.
  • Create group quotation guidelines and review important opportunities individually.
  • Build strategy around fairs, events and high-demand dates.
  • Review contracted rates and distribution partners by actual commercial contribution.
The goal is not maximum occupancy. It is the strongest mix of demand the hotel can realistically attract and serve.
02 / YIELD & PRICING

Price should move with pressure, not habit.

Rate is one of the strongest commercial levers, but it works best when room type, restrictions, booking window and remaining inventory are considered together.

  • Competitor benchmarking and market-position review.
  • Dynamic pricing using pickup, pace and remaining inventory.
  • Restrictions and targeted promotions for strong and weak periods.
  • Room-type yield management and category differentials.
  • Length-of-stay controls where the demand pattern justifies them.
A strong rate decision is not simply higher or lower. It is the rate and condition most likely to maximise the remaining inventory.
03 / FORECASTING

A useful forecast creates time.

It shows strong dates early enough to protect them and weak periods early enough to do something smarter than a last-minute blanket promotion.

  • Historical and advanced pickup models where the data supports them.
  • Daily and weekly pace monitoring against previous periods, budget and target.
  • Monitoring citywide sell-out dates, events and demand spikes.
  • Rolling rooms-revenue forecast for the current and forward months.
  • Monthly performance analysis with explanation of the drivers behind the result.
Forecasting is not about being perfectly right. It is about being useful early enough to make a better decision.
04 / DISTRIBUTION

Gross bookings can hide weak net value.

Each channel has a different audience, cost, promotion structure and ability to create incremental demand. Distribution therefore needs a revenue view, not only a connectivity view.

  • Review OTA visibility and promotions against a defined commercial need.
  • Improve OTA content, room mapping, rate plans and merchandising.
  • Balance direct and indirect demand.
  • Identify leakage, mapping problems and avoidable inconsistencies.
  • Measure commission and promotional dilution so channel performance is understood net of cost.
A channel can look powerful in gross revenue and weak in net contribution. The hotel needs both views.
05 / PERFORMANCE

Reporting should end with a next move.

Performance data is useful when it explains what changed and what the hotel should do next - not when it simply creates a larger report.

  • Occupancy, ADR, RevPAR and rooms-revenue performance.
  • Pickup and pace by stay date, booking window and room category.
  • Business mix and channel contribution.
  • Comparison to last year, same-day-last-year, forecast and budget.
  • Identify dates to protect, dates to stimulate and commercial issues that need attention.
Being ahead of last year does not automatically mean the hotel is performing well. Every result needs context.

FAQ

Revenue management without the jargon.

What is revenue management?

Revenue management uses demand data, booking behaviour, market context and commercial controls to make better decisions about pricing, availability and distribution.

Can remote revenue management work as well as on-site?

Yes, provided the revenue manager has reliable system access, trustworthy data and close communication with the property. Most core revenue work happens through the PMS, distribution systems, market information and regular contact with the hotel team.

What are the main benefits?

More structured pricing, earlier identification of demand changes, stronger forecasting, better control of distribution cost and a clearer understanding of which commercial actions are improving performance.

Does revenue management mean constantly raising prices?

No. It also means knowing when the hotel is overpriced, when demand needs stimulation, when a restriction should be removed and when protecting rate is more valuable than chasing occupancy.
