THE WORK

Increase performance without creating another layer of hotel administration.

Independent and medium-sized hotels often need strong revenue management without needing another full on-site management position. An outsourced model can give the property daily commercial attention while allowing the operational team to stay focused on the hotel and the guest.

The value comes from consistent decision-making: reading pickup, understanding business mix, reacting to market pressure, reviewing the forecast, controlling distribution and checking whether the result is improving for the right reasons.

I work as an extension of the hotel team. That means regular communication, direct access and strategy that fits the actual property rather than a generic revenue template.

Remote revenue management can be highly effective because the core work is driven by data, systems and communication. The important part is not where the revenue manager sits - it is how closely the commercial function understands and works with the hotel.

01

Business mix optimisation

Not all occupancy has the same value. The right mix depends on rate, booking behaviour, cancellation risk, acquisition cost, displacement and what each segment contributes to the hotel over time.

  • Fine-tune the mix between direct, OTA, corporate, groups, wholesale, tour operator and other demand.
  • Identify under-performing segments and practical ways to improve penetration.
  • Create group quotation guidelines and review important group opportunities individually.
  • Build strategy around fairs, events and high-demand dates so valuable inventory is protected.
  • Review corporate and tour-operator pricing and availability.
  • Evaluate distribution partners and contracted rates by actual commercial contribution.
  • Look for lower-cost ways to deliver demand where existing distribution is expensive.
The goal is not the highest possible occupancy. It is the strongest mix of demand the hotel can realistically attract and serve.
02

Yield management & dynamic pricing

Pricing should move with the market, the hotel's remaining inventory and the demand pattern rather than with a fixed calendar. Rate is one of the strongest commercial levers, but it works best when room type, restrictions and booking behaviour are considered at the same time.

  • Competitor benchmarking and market-position review.
  • Dynamic pricing based on pickup, pace, remaining inventory and demand strength.
  • Restrictions and targeted promotions for strong and weak periods.
  • Trend analysis using historical and current booking data.
  • Weekday and weekend price-sensitivity review.
  • Maintain a rolling demand calendar looking at least 12 months ahead.
  • Room-type yield management and category differentials.
  • Length-of-stay controls where demand patterns justify them.
  • Daily pickup analysis with regular strategy adjustment.
A good rate decision is not simply “higher” or “lower”. It is the rate and condition most likely to maximise the value of the remaining inventory.
03

Forecasting & budgeting

A useful forecast creates time. It shows strong dates early enough to protect them and weak periods early enough to respond with something more intelligent than a last-minute blanket discount.

  • Historical and advanced pickup models where the data supports them.
  • Daily and weekly pace monitoring against previous periods, budget and target.
  • Monitoring citywide sell-out dates, events and unusual demand spikes.
  • Rolling rooms-revenue forecast for the current month and forward months.
  • Forecasted occupancy, ADR and revenue with variance to budget and last year.
  • Monthly performance analysis with explanation of the drivers behind the result.
  • Budget support using realistic demand, rate and segment assumptions.
Forecasting is not about being perfectly right. It is about being useful early enough to make a better decision.
04

Distribution & channel control

Distribution is more than keeping availability open. Each channel has a different audience, cost, promotion structure and ability to create incremental demand. Gross reservations can therefore tell a very different story from net contribution.

  • Review OTA visibility and use promotions only where they solve a defined commercial problem.
  • Improve OTA content, room mapping, rate plans and merchandising where necessary.
  • Review GDS productivity and contracted distribution where relevant.
  • Balance direct and indirect demand rather than treating the two as opposing sides.
  • Identify reservation leakage, mapping problems and avoidable rate inconsistencies.
  • Check that PMS, channel manager, booking engine and related systems are configured correctly.
  • Measure commission and promotional dilution so channel performance is understood net of cost.
A channel can look powerful in gross revenue and weak in net value. The commercial view needs both.
05

Performance analysis & next actions

Reporting is useful when it explains what changed and what the hotel should do next. I use performance data to connect the final result back to booking pace, rate decisions, room-type demand and distribution behaviour.

  • Occupancy, ADR, RevPAR and rooms-revenue performance.
  • Pickup and pace by stay date, booking window and room category.
  • Business mix and channel contribution.
  • Comparison to last year, same-day-last-year, forecast and budget.
  • Net ADR and acquisition-cost considerations where channel cost matters.
  • Identify dates to protect, dates to stimulate and commercial issues that need attention.
  • Turn analysis into a short list of practical next actions rather than a bigger report.
Being ahead of last year does not automatically mean the hotel is performing well. Every result needs context.

FAQ

Revenue management, without the jargon.

What is revenue management?

Revenue management uses demand data, booking behaviour, market context and commercial controls to make better decisions about pricing, availability and distribution. In a hotel, the objective is to optimise the value of perishable room inventory over time.

Can remote revenue management work as well as on-site?

Yes, provided the revenue manager has good system access, reliable data and close communication with the property. Most core revenue work is performed through the PMS, distribution systems, market information and regular contact with the hotel team.

What are the main benefits?

More structured pricing, earlier identification of demand changes, stronger forecasting, better control of distribution cost and a clearer understanding of which commercial actions are actually improving performance.

Does revenue management mean constantly raising prices?

No. Strong revenue management also means knowing when the hotel is overpriced, when demand needs stimulation, when a restriction should be removed and when protecting rate is more valuable than chasing occupancy.
