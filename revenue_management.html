THE COMMERCIAL JOB

Read the hotel as one moving system.

Independent and medium-sized hotels often need strong revenue management without adding another full on-site management position. The value of an outsourced model is consistent commercial attention - reading pickup, business mix, pricing, forecast, distribution and the reasons behind the result - while the hotel team remains focused on the property and the guest.

01 / BUSINESS MIX

Not all occupancy has the same value.

The right mix depends on rate, cancellation behaviour, acquisition cost, displacement, booking window and how well the property can actually serve the business.

01Balance direct, OTA, corporate, groups, wholesale and tour-operator demand.
02Create group quotation guidelines and review meaningful opportunities individually.
03Protect events, fairs and high-demand dates before lower-value demand displaces stronger business.
04Review corporate and tour-operator rates by actual contribution, not volume alone.
The target is not maximum occupancy. It is the strongest realistic mix of demand.
02 / FORECASTING

A useful forecast creates time.

Forecasting is not a promise about the future. It is an early-warning system for strong dates, weak periods, changing pace and assumptions that no longer match the market.

01Use historical and advanced pickup models where the data supports them.
02Monitor daily and weekly pace against last year, same-day-last-year, budget and target.
03Track events, citywide compression and unusual demand spikes before they are visible in final occupancy.
04Maintain rolling current-month and forward rooms-revenue forecasts.
Forecasting matters because it creates reaction time.
03 / PRICING & YIELD

Price what is left - not what sold yesterday.

Rate should move with demand pressure, remaining inventory, room-type behaviour and booking window rather than with a static calendar.

01Benchmark the market without blindly copying competitors.
02Use pickup, pace and remaining inventory to move price and restrictions.
03Maintain room-type differentials, length-of-stay controls and a rolling 12-month demand calendar.
04Use targeted promotions only where a real need exists.
A strong rate decision is the rate and condition most likely to maximise the remaining inventory.
04 / DISTRIBUTION

Gross bookings can hide weak net value.

Every channel has a different audience, cost, promotion structure and ability to create incremental demand. Distribution therefore needs a revenue view, not only a connectivity view.

01Review OTA visibility, promotions, room mapping and merchandising against a defined commercial need.
02Review GDS productivity and contracted distribution where relevant.
03Balance direct and indirect demand instead of treating them as opposing sides.
04Identify leakage, mapping problems, rate inconsistencies and unnecessary commission dilution.
The hotel needs to know both gross demand and the value that survives acquisition cost.
05 / PERFORMANCE

The report is finished when the next action is clear.

Occupancy, ADR, RevPAR, pace and channel mix matter when they explain what changed and what the hotel should do next.

01Read occupancy, ADR, RevPAR and rooms revenue together rather than in isolation.
02Compare pickup by stay date, booking window and room category.
03Connect the result back to pricing, business mix, distribution and operational constraints.
04Identify dates to protect, dates to stimulate and issues that need immediate attention.
Being ahead of last year does not automatically mean the hotel is performing well. Every result needs context.

FAQ

Revenue management without the jargon.

Can remote revenue management work as well as on-site?

Yes, provided the revenue manager has reliable system access, trustworthy data and close communication with the property. Most core revenue work happens through the PMS, distribution systems, market information and regular contact with the hotel team.

Does revenue management mean constantly raising prices?

No. It also means knowing when the hotel is overpriced, when demand needs stimulation, when a restriction should be removed and when protecting rate is more valuable than chasing occupancy.

What does the hotel team still need to do?

The best outsourced relationship is collaborative. The hotel provides operational context, group information, local intelligence and feedback while the revenue function turns the data into commercial decisions and keeps the pricing/distribution rhythm moving.
