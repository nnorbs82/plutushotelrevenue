01 / Booking story

Know what the guest needs to understand in five images.

Before the photographer arrives, define what makes each room category worth choosing. Space? View? Layout? Bathroom? Kitchenette? Sofa bed? If the commercial difference is invisible in the imagery, the price difference becomes harder to defend.

Do

Build a shot priority for every sellable category and identify the one feature that earns its price position.

Avoid

Letting the photographer create one generic image set and then reusing it across multiple room categories.

02 / Preparation

The camera notices what operations learns to ignore.

Loose cables, mixed bulbs, creased linen, crooked curtains, half-empty amenity trays and maintenance marks become surprisingly loud in professional images.

  • Deep-clean the exact rooms being photographed.
  • Complete visible maintenance before the shoot.
  • Steam linen and style cushions consistently.
  • Remove bins, cables, signs and operational clutter.
  • Check every lamp and colour temperature.

03 / Coverage

Show enough to remove doubt - not enough to create visual noise.

A guest does not need fifteen near-identical angles. They do need a complete mental model of the room.

Core coverage

Room overview, bed setup, seating/desk, bathroom, storage, entrance and any view/balcony/unique feature.

Commercial gap

If a family room has a sofa bed, a suite has a kitchenette or a room has a restricted view, show it clearly.

04 / Light

Beautiful is good. Believable is better.

Plan the shoot around natural light, but keep exterior views realistic. Over-processing can make a hotel look more impressive for three seconds and less trustworthy once the guest arrives.

  • Schedule rooms for the strongest natural light.
  • Control mixed warm/cool artificial lighting.
  • Retain believable window views.
  • Avoid aggressive HDR and unrealistic skies.

05 / Selection

Sequence images like a booking journey.

Lead with the strongest truthful overview. Then answer the questions that naturally follow: bed, layout, bathroom, view, distinctive features.

  • Use exact images for exact room categories.
  • Check every crop on mobile and OTA layouts.
  • Export efficient WebP versions for the website.
  • Remove near-duplicates.
  • Review the first five images as a set, not individually.
