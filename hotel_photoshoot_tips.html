value.

A hotel photo library should create desire without creating a false product. The strongest images communicate atmosphere, scale, comfort and value before the guest ever enters the room.

THE CONTACT SHEET11FRAMES / ONE VISUAL JOURNEY
01

Scout before the camera comes out.

Walk the hotel in advance. Check guest rooms, suites, lobby, restaurant, bar, meeting spaces, pool, wellness, gardens, terraces and exterior. Identify the features that deserve attention and the places where light, angle or background may create problems.

Note window orientation, reflections, view conditions and when public spaces are naturally quiet enough to shoot.

PRACTICAL FRAMETake quick phone references during the scout and note the best time for every important space.
02

Photograph the hotel's character - not generic luxury.

A city hotel, independent boutique property and resort should not be photographed as if they are the same product. Luxury may come from space and calm; a boutique hotel may depend on art, material and personality; a resort needs to make leisure tangible.

The final set should feel like one property, not a folder of unrelated pictures.

PRACTICAL FRAMEAsk what a guest should feel after seeing only five images.
03

Use equipment for control, not spectacle.

A modern phone can produce excellent supporting content, but a professional shoot benefits from a mirrorless or DSLR camera, quality lenses and a tripod. A wide-angle lens around 16-35mm is useful for interiors when used carefully.

Exaggerated wide angles can materially misrepresent room size. The objective is not the widest room - it is the strongest truthful frame.

PRACTICAL FRAMEProtect straight architectural lines and room proportions.
04

Light separates documentation from desire.

Open curtains and blinds, switch on lamps that support the mood and watch mixed colour temperatures. Natural daylight usually gives rooms their most believable sense of space, while exteriors often benefit from softer early or late light.

Supplementary light should help the space without making the hotel look artificially staged.

PRACTICAL FRAMEPhotograph important exteriors and public spaces at more than one time of day when the mood changes meaningfully.
05

Build a guest journey, not an inventory catalogue.

Exterior and arrival establish context. The lobby establishes the welcome. The room should communicate sleep, space, bathroom quality and view. Breakfast, restaurant, pool, terrace and wellness explain what happens beyond the bed.

Human presence can suggest experience without dominating the product.

PRACTICAL FRAMEPlan the final image order before the shoot and make sure every important part of the stay appears somewhere.
06

Make every line in the frame intentional.

Furniture, windows and architectural lines can lead attention toward the bed, view or distinctive feature. Symmetry works when the space itself is symmetrical; off-centre composition can feel more natural elsewhere.

Remove cables, bins, cleaning equipment, open cupboards, wrinkled linen and paper notices before shooting.

PRACTICAL FRAMECorrect obvious perspective distortion rather than accepting leaning walls.
07

Give rate-driving amenities their own hero moment.

If the hotel has a pool, spa, rooftop, gym, restaurant, courtyard, kitchenette, meeting room or distinctive view, those features need images strong enough to stand on their own.

Combine wide shots explaining the space with details that communicate material, texture and atmosphere.

PRACTICAL FRAMETreat the features that justify the rate as hero content, not as afterthoughts.
08

Edit for consistency, not fantasy.

Post-production should improve clarity without changing the product. Balance exposure, recover highlights, open shadows and correct colour, but the room should still look like the room.

Unreal skies, extreme HDR and oversaturation can reduce trust when the guest arrives.

PRACTICAL FRAMECompare edited images side by side so the library feels like one hotel.
09

Show the hotel after dark when the product changes.

Façade lighting, bars, restaurants, terraces, city views and pools can become different products after sunset. Night photography matters when evening atmosphere is part of the stay.

A tripod, lower ISO and longer exposures usually produce cleaner results than forcing sensitivity upward.

PRACTICAL FRAMEIf the hotel sells an evening experience, show it.
10

Keep enough image information for future use.

RAW files retain much more information than compressed JPEG files, making it easier to recover highlights, lift shadows and correct white balance in difficult interiors.

Keep a high-quality master library for future campaigns, crops and re-edits even when website/OTA exports are compressed.

PRACTICAL FRAMEExport separate web-ready files rather than repeatedly recompressing the same JPEG.
11

An image library is only an asset if the hotel can use it.

Photographer agreements should clearly define usage rights for website, OTAs, social, paid advertising, press and any limits on territory or duration.

Identifiable guests or models require the appropriate permission, and the same care applies to licensed artwork or third-party material visible in the campaign.

PRACTICAL FRAMEStore usage agreements and releases together with the master image library.

Create desire without making the real stay feel like a downgrade.
