Photograph the stay, not just the furniture.

01

Define the booking story first

Before anyone picks up a camera, decide what a guest must understand from the first few images. For most hotels that means the room standard, the sense of space, the bed, the bathroom, the view or location and one memorable feature.

  • Choose one hero image for every sellable room type.
  • Identify the real reason a guest would pay more for the next category.
  • Plan both landscape and mobile-friendly crops.
Do

Brief the photographer by room category and booking question.

Avoid

Starting with a generic list like “bed, bathroom, lobby, breakfast”.

02

Prepare the room harder than you think

Photography magnifies small operational details. A cable on the floor, creased duvet, mismatched bulb or half-open curtain becomes part of the product presentation.

  • Complete visible maintenance before shoot day.
  • Steam linen and align furniture consistently.
  • Remove bins, loose cables, signage and operational clutter.
  • Check every lamp and keep colour temperatures consistent.
Do

Walk the room exactly as a guest would and correct everything visible.

Avoid

Assuming retouching will fix preparation problems later.

03

Build a complete shot list by category

A room gallery should remove uncertainty. One beautiful corner image is not enough if the guest still cannot understand the layout, bathroom or bed configuration.

  • One clear room overview from the most useful angle.
  • Bed, seating, work area, storage and entrance where relevant.
  • Bathroom overview plus important features such as bath or walk-in shower.
  • View, terrace, kitchenette or connecting feature where applicable.
04

Use light deliberately - but keep it believable

The goal is not to make the room look like a different property. Schedule each space when natural light works best and expose the exterior so the view still looks real.

  • Plan east/west-facing rooms at different times of day.
  • Control mixed warm and cool artificial light.
  • Keep skies, greenery and windows natural.
  • Avoid aggressive HDR and over-bright interiors.
Do

Aim for polished and inviting while keeping the room recognisable on arrival.

Avoid

Making every window pure white or every room unnaturally bright.

05

Select images for conversion, not for the photographer's portfolio

The strongest creative image is not always the strongest first booking image. Lead with the photo that gives the guest the most useful and attractive understanding of the room.

  • Remove near-duplicates.
  • Put exact room-category images before generic hotel images.
  • Review how every image crops on mobile and OTA cards.
  • Export responsive WebP versions for the hotel website.

Hotel photoshoot checklist

The version I would use before confirming a shoot is ready.

✓Every sellable category has a defined hero image.
✓Room differences are visible, not only described.
✓Maintenance and deep cleaning are complete.
✓Linen, curtains and lighting are consistent.
✓Bathrooms and practical features are covered.
✓Exterior views remain believable.
✓Mobile crops have been considered.
✓The final sequence removes duplicate images.
