Photography field guide

Show the stay.
Not an empty room.

The guest should understand atmosphere, space, light and value before they read the room description. This is how I brief a hotel photoshoot from a commercial point of view.

01

Start with the booking story

Before anyone picks up a camera, decide what a guest must understand from the first five images. A beautiful photograph that creates the wrong expectation can hurt conversion just as much as a bad photograph.

Do

Show scale, bed setup, bathroom quality, view/location and one memorable detail.

Avoid

Five nearly identical bed shots that tell the guest nothing new.

02

Prepare harder than you shoot

A lens notices everything: cables, bins, wrinkled linen, crooked lampshades, dirty windows and mismatched light temperatures. Fix the room before trying to fix the image.

  • Deep clean and complete maintenance first
  • Steam linen and simplify surfaces
  • Use a repeatable styling standard across room categories
03

Give every category its own truth

If a guest buys a City Twin, show the City Twin. If the smaller room has a limitation, photograph it honestly and compensate with the genuine strengths of that category.

Do

Create a shot list by sellable room category.

Avoid

Using your best suite photographs to represent every room type.

04

Light should feel believable

Bright is good. Fake is not. Keep exterior views natural, control mixed indoor colour temperatures and schedule each space when its daylight works in your favour.

05

Edit for a booking journey

The gallery is a sequence, not a storage folder. Start strong, answer the biggest guest questions early and remove duplicates.

  • Hero image
  • Room overview
  • Bed and living area
  • Bathroom
  • View / balcony / special feature
  • Useful detail
