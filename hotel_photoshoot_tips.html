Hotel photography

Photograph what the guest is trying to decide.

A hotel photoshoot is not an interior-design award submission. Its job is to make the room, the experience and the differences between categories easy to understand before the guest books.

01

Start with the booking story, not the camera.

Before the photographer arrives, decide what a guest must understand in the first five images. For most rooms that means scale, bed setup, bathroom standard, natural light/view and one distinctive reason to choose the category.

Do
  • Define one selling point per category
  • Plan a hero shot for each room type
  • Show meaningful differences between categories
Avoid
  • Twenty photos of the same corner
  • Decor details before the room overview
  • Using the same images for different room types
02

Photography magnifies every operational detail.

A cable, wrinkled duvet or half-open wardrobe door can be invisible during a room inspection and painfully obvious in a professional image. Prepare the room as if the photo will be used for the next five years - because it might be.

Prepare
  • Deep clean and finish maintenance first
  • Steam linen and align bedside items
  • Hide bins, cables and operational clutter
Watch for
  • Mixed lamp colour temperatures
  • Empty soap dispensers or tired amenities
  • Reflections of equipment or people
03

A shot list stops expensive gaps.

Build the list by sellable category, not by floor. Every room type should have a complete minimum set so the guest does not need to guess about the bathroom, seating, view, sofa bed or balcony.

Minimum room set
  • Wide room overview
  • Bed and sleeping setup
  • Bathroom overview
  • View / balcony if relevant
Useful extras
  • Desk or seating area
  • Kitchenette and equipment
  • Storage / wardrobe
  • Connecting-room setup
04

Bright does not mean believable.

Good hotel photography should feel attractive without making the guest suspicious when they arrive. Plan rooms for the time of day they receive their best natural light and keep windows and exterior views credible.

Aim for
  • Natural-looking daylight
  • Balanced interior/exterior exposure
  • Consistent warmth between rooms
Avoid
  • White windows with no visible view
  • Orange lamps against blue daylight
  • HDR that makes the room look unreal
05

The order of the images is part of the sale.

The strongest truthful room overview should normally lead. Then answer questions in a logical sequence. The guest should not need to scroll through eight lobby details before seeing the bathroom of the room they are considering.

Useful sequence
  • Room hero
  • Second angle / layout
  • Bathroom
  • View or key feature
Conversion killers
  • Near-duplicate first images
  • Wrong category photography
  • Beautiful but misleading wide-angle distortion

Before the photographer leaves

Hotel photoshoot checklist

  • Every sellable room category photographed
  • Bathroom shown for every meaningful category
  • Bed configuration is obvious
  • Sofa beds / extra sleeping setup documented
  • Room view captured where relevant
  • Public spaces photographed at their best time
  • No cables, bins or operational clutter
  • Exterior light and windows look natural
  • Desktop and mobile hero crops considered
  • File names identify exact room category
  • WebP exports planned for website use
  • Final sequence reviewed from a guest perspective
