Tourism and Global Warming: A Delicate Balance

07 SEP 2024 · PLUTUS HOTEL REVENUE

Tourism supports jobs, local businesses, cultural exchange and investment in destinations around the world. At the same time, transport, energy use, construction, food systems and visitor pressure all contribute to an environmental footprint that is increasingly difficult to separate from the future of the industry.

The relationship is two-way

Travel contributes to emissions, but climate change also changes travel demand. Heatwaves, wildfires, water stress, unreliable snow seasons and changing coastlines can alter when and where guests want to travel.

For hotels, this means sustainability is not only a communications topic. It can influence operating costs, seasonality, insurance, destination demand and long-term asset value.

Accommodation has practical levers

Hotels can reduce energy and water use, improve building controls, change purchasing decisions, reduce food waste and make housekeeping practices more efficient. The exact priorities depend on the property, but measurement matters. A hotel cannot manage a resource it never tracks.

The guest experience does not need to suffer. Many efficient systems - better heating control, LED lighting, smart maintenance and reduced waste - can improve both the operation and the stay.

Avoid sustainability theatre

Travellers are increasingly able to recognise vague environmental claims. A hotel is better served by communicating a small number of specific actions than presenting itself as “green” without evidence.

If a certification is used, keep it current. If the hotel asks guests to reuse towels, explain the broader policy rather than making the guest feel solely responsible for the property's environmental impact.

Destinations need balance too

Overtourism can place pressure on housing, transport, public space and local ecosystems. Hotels operate inside that destination relationship. Supporting local suppliers, spreading demand into quieter periods and helping guests discover less congested areas can create value for both the visitor and the place.

Commercial strategy and responsibility can align

Reducing energy waste lowers cost. Extending demand into shoulder seasons can improve revenue while reducing pressure on peak periods. Local partnerships can create distinctive guest experiences while keeping more visitor spend in the destination.

The useful question is not whether sustainability and revenue management are opposites. It is where smarter resource use, better demand distribution and a stronger guest proposition can support both.
